## Overview

Independtenly working on a second iteration of the B.A.R.C. Walking Robot. 
B.A.R.C. Mk 2.0 will feature a full redesign, including an upgraded drivetrain,
four additional legs for improved stability, and a cleaner final product.

## Design Revisions From Mk 1.0

B.A.R.C. Mk 1.0 was designed as a radio-controlled quadrupedal walking robot, and
was largely successful as a first iteration. This being said, there was certainly room
for imrpovement.

## Leg System Revisions

A challenge that the Mk 1.0 encountered was the weight of the body. With all of the
electronics being stored within the body of the robot, the complex jansen-linkage
style legs sturggled to operate properly. This led to instability in the robot as well
as duress on the drivetrain and motor.

For this reason, Mk 2.0 will feature eight legs instead of the original four. This will
improve stability of the robot, as it will ensure that one leg is always on the ground at
each "corner" of the robot. This change will also place less strain on the drivetrain, as
in Mk 1.0 large amounts of strain were the result of the robot tipping onto whichever "corner"
of the robot had a leg that was lifted, at which point the leg would have to push the entire
robot back up into its standing position.

## Drivetrain Revisions

Mk 1.0 featured a quite complex drivetrain. This made the robot difficult to assemble, and
was not very visually appealing.

To remedy this, I am designing an entirely new drivetrain for B.A.R.C., featuring
planetary gearboxes for reductions in speed and increased torque, as well as more
accessible motor placement. I have also added large spur gears on the front and back
of the robot. These function as yet another gear reduction, and also just look pretty
neat.

## Splitting Up the Work

One of the main issues with Mk 1.0 was that the vast majority of gears and electronic
components were located at the back of the robot. The intention was to keep the weight
on the front of the robot to reduce strain on the steering system.

In Mk 2.0, I have split the robot into two sections, the Steerer and the Driver.
The Steerer side will contain the motor and all gears and systems used to steer the
robot. The Driver side will contain the motor and the vast majority of the drivetrain.

Better distributing weight across the full body of the robot will improve stability while
walking, and reduce the load any one side must endure.

## Current Stage

I am currently working on refining the design of B.A.R.C. Mk 2.0. Although much of the
initial design has been completed, many of the details still need to be worked out.
In this iteration, I am also working to design print-in-place models for the legs and
body of the robot. Being able to 3D-print the legs pre-assembled would be a huge time-saver
and would ensure that they are assembled correctely each and every time.

## What This Demonstrates.

This project is a great example of my ability to diagnose design issues and develop effective
solutions. It also demonstrates my understanding of mechanical systems: I am currently
working on designing a modular and stackable planetary gearbox system that will be used
to refine the robot's drivetrain and improve torque output by the drive motor. No project is
ever really finished, there is always room for improvements.
