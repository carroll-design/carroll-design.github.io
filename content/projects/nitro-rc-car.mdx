## Overview

Performed a complete teardown and rebuild of the transmission on a Traxxas Slayer Pro 4x4, including disassembly, inspection, cleaning, reassembly, and adjustment of the drivetrain components.

The project included rebuilding the two-speed transmission and replacing the stock slipper clutch with an upgraded heavy-duty slipper clutch assembly.

## Identifying the Problem

It was first noticed that the vehicle was having trouble producing torque, despite the engine still running at full power. After further inspection it was determined that the vehicle was stuck in second gear, and the failure was likely a result of the automatic transmission.

## Removing the Transmission

Getting to the transmission would prove to be quite difficult, as it was located underneath several layers of protection as well as other components. Ultimately, I would have to remove the underbody skid plate as well as the surrounding drivetrain components to get to.

After removing any components which were either blocking or attached to the transmission, I was able to remove the transmission entirely from the chassis and work on the internal geartrain independently of the rest of the vehicle.

I kept track of all fasteners, shafts, and other components that were displaced during this process and throughout the rest of the project, ensuring that I could later properly reassemble the vehicle.

## Opening the Transmission

Now that the transmission was free from the chassis, it was time to open it up and see what the problem was.

Upon opening the transmission, it became apparent that some of the gears within had become misaligned. This was likely a result of the sick jumps and endless bashing bestowed upon the vehicle.

<ProjectPhoto
  src="/images/projects/nitro-rc-car/transmission-gears-cleaned.webp"

It is very important that the gears within the transmission mesh properly, as they transfer power throughout the entire drivetrain. I proceeded to fully disassemble the transmission, carefully noting where each gear went.

During this process, I was able to gain a deeper understanding of the transmission mechanically. Seeing each of the gears, shafts, and other components and how they all interacted gave me a new, hands-on perspective into mechanical systems.

## Reassembling the Transmission

Once each of the transmission's internals were inspected, I began to rebuild the transmission.

The transmission consists of several gears operating in very close proximity to one another. When reassembling, I carefully ensured that each gear and shaft was in the correct position and orientation, and that they could spin freely while meshing with the appropriate gears. I took extra care during this process because if any one of the gears was installed incorrectly, it could prevent the entire drivetrain from functioning.

## Repairing the Slipper Clutch

With the transmission removed from the chassis, I was better able to see the slipper clutch, located on the side of the transmission. Upon inspection, it was noted that the friction pads were quite worn, and one of the three pads was missing.

To fix this, I ordered a slipper clutch rebuild kit. The kit I selected was the Traxxas 5352R Heavy-Duty Slipper Clutch Rebuild Kit. This kit was selected because it was the correct fit for this specific vehicle, and also had aluminum friction pads which would serve as a more resilient alternative to the factory friction pads.

During this process I thoroughly researched slipper clutches, which was a concept I had previously known little about. In my research I learned about their function; providing controlled slip between the output of the engine and the drivetrain. I was then able to put this knowledge to work, diagnosing the failure, deciding on a course of action, and implementing the fixes.

## Reassembling the Complete Transmission with Slipper Clutch

Now that the transmission was reassembled, I could reattach and reassemble the slipper clutch.

During reassembly, I paid close attention to the position and orientation of each of the components and made sure that the slipper clutch moved properly. I also checked the transmission's output and input shafts and ensured that they too moved properly.

## Reinstalling the Transmission

Once the reassembly of the transmission was verified, it was time to reinstall it to the chassis.

I reconnected the drivetrain components and secured the transmission to the chasis. During and after this process, I routinely checked to make sure that everything was moving and otherwise working as it should. Once all components were reinstalled, it was time to take it for a spin.

## What This Demonstrates

This project gave me hands-on experience working with a multi-stage mechanical power transmission, from individual components through a fully integrated drivetrain. The work required identifying and diagnosing mechanical failures, an understanding of mechanical systems, determining a course of action, and carefully implementing the fixes.
